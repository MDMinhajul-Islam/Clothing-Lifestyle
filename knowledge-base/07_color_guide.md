# Color Guide

## Describing Color

Color names can vary between collections. Use the product’s current color name when discussing a specific item.

## Neutral Colors

Black, white, ivory, cream, beige, camel, taupe, gray, brown, and navy can support versatile wardrobes. Neutral does not mean colorless; undertone and texture influence the result.

## Warm and Cool Undertones

Warm colors tend toward yellow, orange, or red. Cool colors tend toward blue, green, or violet. Many shades sit between these groups.

## Tonal Dressing

Tonal outfits combine related shades within one color family. Variation in texture and value can add depth.

## Complementary Contrast

Colors opposite one another can create strong contrast. Use one as the focus and the other as an accent when a quieter result is preferred.

## Analogous Combinations

Neighboring colors often create a cohesive palette, such as blue with blue-green or red with red-violet.

## Practical Combinations

- Black pairs easily with neutrals, saturated colors, and metallic accents.
- Navy works well with white, gray, camel, burgundy, and soft blue.
- Brown coordinates with cream, blue, olive, rust, and muted pink.
- White can sharpen tailoring or lighten layered outfits.
- Denim blue works with most neutrals and many warm or bright accents.

## Digital Color

Photography, lighting, and display calibration can affect appearance. Product media should be treated as guidance rather than a perfect color standard.
