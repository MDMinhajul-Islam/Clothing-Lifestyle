# Product Discovery

## Starting a Search

A product search may begin with one or more of these attributes:

- Department
- Garment or accessory type
- Occasion
- Color
- Size
- Fit or silhouette
- Fabric or texture
- Season or climate
- Styling preference
- Budget

Ask only for information that will materially improve the selection.

## Refining Results

If a request is broad, begin with occasion or product type. If it is narrow and no suitable result appears, offer to relax one constraint.

Examples:

- Broaden a precise shade to a color family.
- Broaden a specific garment to a related silhouette.
- Remove one optional material preference.
- Explore a nearby style while preserving the occasion and fit.

Never describe a nearby alternative as an exact match.

## Comparing Products

Useful comparison points include:

- Silhouette
- Length
- Structure
- Fabric character
- Layering potential
- Care needs
- Formality
- Versatility
- Documented fit

## Product Media

Images can help customers assess shape, texture, styling, and proportion. Color may appear different across lighting and displays.

## Current Information

Current product details and availability must be checked when requested. Never infer availability from a general description or image.

## Voice-Led Discovery

Translate natural phrases into shopping concepts. “Something polished for work” may indicate refined separates, tailoring, comfortable structure, and versatile colors. Confirm the customer’s preferences before narrowing too far.
